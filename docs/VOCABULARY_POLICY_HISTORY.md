## 3. Original paragraph: vocabulary exclusion and term-level suppression list

**Vocabulary exclusion and term-level suppression list (Methods 3.3).** Seven symbols (MICE, DCR, PC, C2, FBN1, SGCG, WDHD1) are excluded from all rankings entirely (`GENE_EXCLUDE`). MICE, DCR, PC and C2 produce common-word or abbreviation collisions with no safer alias. FBN1, SGCG and WDHD1 were inherited from the live application's earlier quality-control policy and were not individually evaluated when the pipeline was built, so they were tested post hoc (`scripts/analysis/gene_exclusion_readmission_test.py`): the primary ranking was re-run for all 19 cancers with the exclusion lifted. Admitting every term of the three genes changed no top-10 slot and 208 of 950 top-50 slots, all through WDHD1, which entered the top 50 of nine cancers (Bladder, Breast, Cervical, Colorectal, Esophageal, Leukemia, Liver, Lung and Pancreatic Cancer, at ranks 18 to 47) and the top 10 of none. The cause is its alias AND-1, which normalises to the phrase "and 1" (about 6,800 of 250,061 Breast abstracts contain it, against 2 for the symbol WDHD1); suppressing the bare symbol made no difference. Admitting only the approved symbol and approved full name of the three genes, with their aliases removed (AND-1, CTF4 and CHTF4 for WDHD1; OCTD for FBN1; SCARMD2, DAGA4, DMDA and MGC130048 for SGCG), left all 950 top-50 slots unchanged, and none of the three genes entered any top-50 list. The exclusions therefore act as an alias-collision control and do not alter the reported rankings. A separate set of 13 symbols is instead handled by term-level suppression (`suppress_ambiguous_symbols()`), under which only the bare, ambiguous symbol is removed as a matchable term, while the gene's full approved name and every other alias remain matchable under the same identity. This paper's audit was subsequently adopted into the live application, which now shows users side-by-side Raw and Vetted gene results, the live site's own analogue of this paper's true-raw versus cleaned vocabulary comparison. OncoDigger's `gene_quality.py` policy version 2026-10-05-v2 implements this 13-symbol suppression set, the four safer-alias substitutions below, and the MET/FH negative-context corrections (Methods 3.3) without modification, as part of a broader live policy that additionally suppresses further symbols not evaluated in this paper. Its `GENE_EXCLUDE` additionally blanket-excludes REST, whereas this paper corrected REST via a Thyroid-specific postings removal rather than a global exclusion (Results 5.2). Four of the 13 were originally identified and are additionally indexed under a distinct safer alias, namely GC (colliding with "gastric cancer") under VDBP, HR ("hazard ratio") under KDM3D, HCCS ("hepatocellular carcinoma") under CCHL, and BPIFA4P under LATH. The remaining nine were found by a systematic post-hoc sweep of every gene symbol of four characters or fewer appearing anywhere in the top-50 rankings (66 candidates), each verified against live PubMed titles and abstracts. MB (myoglobin) matches MDA-MB-231/MDA-MB-436 cell lines, "mutations per megabase," and "medulloblastoma." ELN (elastin) matches "European LeukemiaNet." CP (ceruloplasmin) matches "chronic phase" and "chronic pancreatitis." UBC (ubiquitin C) matches "urothelial bladder cancer." CAMP (cathelicidin antimicrobial peptide) matches "cyclic AMP." EFS (embryonal Fyn-associated substrate) matches "event-free survival." IVD (isovaleryl-CoA dehydrogenase) matches "in vitro diagnostic." SCT (secretin) matches "solid and cystic tumor" of the pancreas. SON matches the English word "son." Two further candidates surfaced by the same sweep, INS and TPO, were checked against live PubMed but not confirmed as false aliases with comparable clarity, and were left unmodified in the vocabulary rather than acted on with inconclusive evidence. These 13 symbols are the original term-level set. The later corrections extend it (policy v4 and v5, below), so the final policy excludes 7 genes and suppresses 31 bare symbols and 51 single-word aliases at term level. Four of the 31 symbols (GC, HR, HCCS and BPIFA4P) have no entry in this paper's base vocabulary, so 78 of the 82 declared terms change the matcher. The analysis scripts that read the application's unfiltered lookup table (Methods 3.4, Supplementary Results S7) import `gene_quality.py` policy version 2026-09-27-v1 (commit `d592370` of the live-application repository), vendored in `reference_engine_code/gene_quality_test_engine/`, not the later 2026-10-05-v2 policy described above.

## 4. Original paragraph: downstream impact of the nine newly-found false aliases

**Downstream impact of the nine newly-found false aliases (Methods 3.3, Results 5.1-5.3).** This vocabulary correction was made before this paper's conversion to IntOGen as the primary reference. All numbers reported in this paper come from the final pipeline of Methods 3.2 and 3.3, and the only earlier figures quoted are the v1.1 release values in the background-count paragraph below. These nine were first corrected via a blunt, symbol-level exclusion (matching the treatment already used for the original 7), then this was itself refined to the term-level suppression policy described above, since blunt exclusion treated these nine more harshly than the four originally-identified symbols. SCT alone among the nine recovers a genuine match once only its bare symbol is suppressed rather than the whole gene excluded, since "secretin" independently earns Pancreatic Cancer rank 42 on the 2026-09-27 build.

## 5. Original paragraph: collision screen on the corrected rankings

**Collision screen on the corrected rankings (Methods 3.3).** The predeclared screen of the vocabulary audit (`scripts/analysis/vocab_collision_risk_screen.py`; a gene is RED if fewer than 50% of its matched papers contain the standalone capitalised symbol, AMBER if 50% to 85% do or fewer than 30% of its matches lie near a generic qualifier word, and GREEN otherwise, with the same thresholds and the same generic qualifier list for every gene) was rerun on the 347 genes of the exact-background top-50 lists, before the v4 and v5 corrections, and gave 268 GREEN, 61 AMBER and 18 RED. Of the 36 genes new to any top-50 list (ACD, ACVR1, APOBEC3B, AXIN2, BTG2, CCS, CD58, CUL3, DDR2, ERCC1, FAM107B, FANCC, FAT4, FHIT, ID3, IL6ST, KALRN, KLF6, MLH3, MT-ATP6, MUC2, PREX2, PTPRD, PTPRT, RBM10, RHO, RPL22, SETBP1, SHE, SMO, STAT6, TFEB, TNF, TRAF2, UBA1 and USP8), 19 are IntOGen drivers. Two were RED (RHO and SHE) and eight were AMBER (AXIN2, CCS, FAM107B, ID3, MT-ATP6, PTPRD, SMO and TRAF2). Eight sampled abstracts each for SHE, RHO and CCS contained no mention of the gene (the pronoun, Rho GTPases and Spearman's rho, and abbreviations such as colorectal cancer screening and Charlson comorbidity score). A further 30 abstracts each for RHO and CCS contained 0 and 3 genuine mentions, the 3 being the copper chaperone gene CCS, while the other 27 CCS abstracts used the abbreviation for Cronkhite-Canada syndrome, colon cancers, scoring systems and similar terms, and the 30 RHO abstracts concerned Rho GTPases or Spearman's rho. Conversely, all sampled abstracts for ID3, SMO, PTPRD, AXIN2 and TRAF2 (10, 10, 7, 5 and 8) were genuine mentions of the gene (for example mutations in Burkitt lymphoma, the hedgehog pathway component Smoothened in medulloblastoma, and PTPRD inactivation in melanoma). The abstracts were drawn at random with a fixed seed from the collection in which the gene ranks, and were read by a language-model agent that had seen the manuscript, so this is not independent validation. FAM107B and MT-ATP6 each have a single matched paper and were not read.
